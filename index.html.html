📚 Why use a mortgage calculator?

A mortgage calculator helps you test "what if" questions before you talk to a lender. What if I put 10% down instead of 20%? What if I choose a 15-year loan? What if I pay an extra $200 each month? Each change shows you the effect on your payment and total interest, so you can compare options side by side.

Many buyers are surprised by the total monthly cost. The loan payment is only one part. Property tax and homeowners insurance are often added to the bill through an escrow account, and in some cases private mortgage insurance (PMI) is added too. Our calculator includes taxes and insurance so you see a realistic number from the start.

Remember that calculators give estimates. Your actual loan terms depend on your credit score, income, debts, property location and the lender you choose. Use the numbers as a planning tool, then confirm them with a Loan Estimate from a lender.

📖 How to use this calculator

Start with the home price and your down payment. The calculator subtracts the down payment from the price to find your loan amount, then estimates your monthly principal and interest using the rate and term you enter.

Next, add your yearly property tax and homeowners insurance. Divide each by 12 to get the monthly amount, which is added to your principal and interest. Together, these make up your total monthly housing cost, known as PITI.

Finally, enter an extra monthly payment to see how it changes your results. The calculator applies the extra money straight to principal every month and shows how much interest you save and how many months you finish early.

Tip: Try a few scenarios, such as a 15-year term, a lower rate or a bigger down payment, and compare the results.

🧩 What makes up your monthly payment?

Principal

Principal is the amount you borrowed. Each payment reduces this balance a little. In the early years, only a small part of your payment goes to principal.

Interest

Interest is what the lender charges for lending you money. It is calculated on your remaining balance each month, so it decreases as your balance falls.

Property tax

Local governments charge property tax based on the assessed value of your home. Rates vary a lot by county and city. Many lenders collect one-twelfth of the yearly tax each month and pay the bill for you from an escrow account.

Homeowners insurance

Insurance protects your home against damage and covers liability. Lenders require it, and it is usually paid through escrow as well. Some areas also need separate flood or wind coverage.

🚀 How extra payments help you

Mortgage interest is calculated on your outstanding balance. When you pay extra toward principal, the balance drops sooner, so less interest builds up in every month that follows. Over a long loan, that adds up to a lot of money.

Extra payments can also shorten your loan term. Your regular payment stays the same, but you finish the loan earlier. Many borrowers use small, regular extra payments, such as an additional $100 or $200 a month, or a yearly lump sum from a bonus or tax refund.

Before you start, check three things with your lender. First, confirm that extra payments go to principal and not to future payments. Second, confirm there is no prepayment penalty. Third, ask whether you can make extra payments online or need to send them in a specific way.

⚖️ 15-year vs 30-year mortgage

A 15-year mortgage usually has a lower interest rate than a 30-year loan, and the monthly payment is higher because you repay the loan in half the time. Over the life of the loan, you pay far less interest and build equity faster.

A 30-year mortgage has lower required monthly payments. That leaves more room in your budget for savings, emergencies and other goals. Many people choose a 30-year loan and then make extra payments when their income allows, which gives them a lower required payment with the option to pay it off faster.

The right choice depends on your income stability, savings, other debts and long-term plans. If you can comfortably afford the higher payment and want to be debt-free sooner, a 15-year loan can be a strong choice.

🏡 How much house can you afford?

A common guideline is the 28/36 rule. It suggests that your housing costs (PITI) should stay below 28% of your gross monthly income, and your total monthly debt payments, including housing, car loans, student loans and credit cards, should stay below 36%.

Lenders may allow higher ratios, especially for borrowers with strong credit and savings. However, a lower ratio gives you breathing room for repairs, job changes and rising costs. Remember that property taxes and insurance can increase over time.

Don't forget upfront costs. Closing costs are usually between 2% and 5% of the loan amount, and you may also need money for moving, furniture and repairs. Keep an emergency fund after you pay your down payment and closing costs.

💵 Down payment and upfront costs

How much down payment do I need to buy a house?

It depends on the loan type. Conventional loans can start at 3% to 5% down for some buyers, FHA loans require at least 3.5% with a qualifying credit score, and VA and USDA loans may allow zero down for eligible borrowers. A larger down payment lowers your loan amount, your monthly payment and often your interest rate. Ask a lender which programs you qualify for.

Is 20% down really necessary?

No, 20% is not required for most loans. However, with a conventional loan, putting less than 20% down usually means you pay private mortgage insurance (PMI) until you reach enough equity. Many buyers choose a smaller down payment to keep savings for emergencies, and that can be a sensible decision if the monthly cost is manageable.

What are closing costs and how much will they be?

Closing costs are fees paid when the loan finishes. They often include the lender's origination fee, appraisal, title insurance, title search, recording fees, credit report fees, prepaid interest and prepaid insurance or tax deposits. They are commonly around 2% to 5% of the loan amount. Some sellers or lenders offer credits that can reduce what you pay upfront.

Can I use gift money for my down payment?

Many loan programs allow gift funds from a close family member, as long as the donor provides a signed letter stating the money is a gift and does not need to be repaid. Lenders usually want to see bank statements showing where the money came from. Rules vary by loan type, so confirm the details with your lender early.

What is an earnest money deposit?

Earnest money is a good-faith deposit you give when your offer on a home is accepted. It is usually 1% to 3% of the purchase price, and it is held in escrow and applied to your closing costs or down payment at closing. If you back out for a reason not allowed in your contract, you may lose it. Read the contingencies carefully.

📈 Interest rates and loan terms

What is the difference between interest rate and APR?

The interest rate is the cost of borrowing the principal. The annual percentage rate (APR) includes the interest rate plus certain fees and costs, such as points and some closing charges, spread over the life of the loan. The APR is usually higher than the rate and helps you compare offers more fairly. The calculator above uses the interest rate for the payment estimate.

What are mortgage points, and are they worth buying?

A discount point is a fee paid upfront, usually equal to 1% of the loan amount, that lowers your interest rate. Points can make sense if you plan to keep the loan for many years. To check, divide the cost of the points by the monthly savings to find the break-even period. If you expect to sell or refinance before that point, the points may not pay off.

Does my credit score affect my mortgage rate?

Yes. Lenders use your credit score to judge risk, and a higher score usually qualifies you for a lower rate. Even a small difference in rate can change your payment by a noticeable amount over 30 years. Before applying, check your credit report for errors, pay bills on time and try to keep credit card balances low.

Should I choose a fixed-rate or adjustable-rate mortgage?

A fixed-rate mortgage keeps the same interest rate and principal and interest payment for the whole term, which makes budgeting simple. An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) starts with a fixed rate for a set number of years, then adjusts based on a market index. ARMs can have lower starting rates, but payments can rise later. They can suit buyers who plan to move or refinance before the adjustment period, but they carry more risk.

Is a 15-year or 30-year mortgage better?

It depends on your goals and budget. A 15-year loan has higher monthly payments but usually a lower rate and far less total interest. A 30-year loan has lower required payments and more flexibility. Many people take the 30-year option and pay extra when they can, which keeps the lower required payment as a safety net.

🏦 Loan types

What is PMI and how do I get rid of it?

Private mortgage insurance (PMI) protects the lender if you stop paying. It is usually required on conventional loans when your down payment is under 20%. PMI is often added to your monthly payment. Under federal rules for most conventional loans, you can ask to cancel PMI once your balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, and it is generally removed automatically at 78%. Check your loan documents for the exact terms, and ask about options if your home value has risen.

What is an FHA loan?

An FHA loan is backed by the Federal Housing Administration. It is designed for buyers with lower credit scores or smaller down payments, and it commonly requires as little as 3.5% down with a qualifying credit score. FHA loans include mortgage insurance premiums, both upfront and annual, and these generally stay for the life of the loan unless you refinance. Compare the total cost with a conventional loan before you decide.

Who is eligible for a VA loan?

VA loans are offered to eligible veterans, active-duty service members, certain reservists and some surviving spouses. They often require no down payment and no monthly PMI. A VA funding fee is usually charged, although it can be reduced or waived for some borrowers. Eligibility depends on service history, so check with the Department of Veterans Affairs or a VA-approved lender.

What is a jumbo loan?

A jumbo loan is a mortgage that is larger than the limits set for conforming loans by the Federal Housing Finance Agency. Because these loans are not backed by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, lenders may require higher credit scores, larger down payments and more cash reserves. Loan limits change each year, so check the current figure for your county.

What is pre-approval and why does it matter?

Pre-approval is a lender's conditional commitment to lend you a specific amount, based on a review of your income, assets, debts and credit. It usually involves submitting documents and a credit check. Sellers often prefer offers from pre-approved buyers because it shows you are serious and financially ready. Pre-approval is different from pre-qualification, which is a quicker estimate with less verification.

🧾 Monthly costs and escrow

What is an escrow account?

An escrow account is managed by your lender or loan servicer. Each month you pay one-twelfth of your estimated yearly property tax and homeowners insurance along with your principal and interest. The servicer holds that money and pays the tax and insurance bills when they are due. Escrow protects the property and makes large annual bills easier to manage, but the monthly amount can change if taxes or premiums go up.

Why did my escrow payment go up?

Escrow amounts are reviewed each year. If property taxes or homeowners insurance premiums increased, your monthly payment may rise. Sometimes an escrow shortage also occurs when the account did not collect enough during the past year, and the servicer may spread the shortage across future payments. Your annual escrow statement explains the change, and you can ask the servicer to walk you through it.

How can I lower my property taxes?

Many areas let homeowners appeal their property assessment if they believe it is too high. You usually need evidence, such as recent sales of similar homes, a professional appraisal or documented problems with the property. Check your local assessor's office for deadlines and forms. Some states and counties also offer homestead exemptions that reduce the taxable value for owner-occupants.

How much homeowners insurance do I need?

Lenders require enough coverage to protect the loan, which generally means insuring the home's rebuild cost, not its market value. The land is not included in the rebuild cost. Consider liability coverage and extra protection for valuable items. In flood or hurricane-prone areas, you may need separate policies. Compare quotes each year, because premiums can change significantly.

Does a mortgage calculator include all my costs?

Our calculator includes principal, interest, property tax and insurance, and it lets you add an extra payment. It does not include PMI, HOA fees, closing costs, utilities, maintenance or repairs. These can add a lot to your total monthly and yearly cost. Include them in your budget when you decide how much house you can afford.

🔁 Refinancing and paying off early

When does refinancing make sense?

Refinancing replaces your current mortgage with a new one. People often do it to get a lower interest rate, shorten the loan term, switch from an adjustable to a fixed rate, or remove PMI. Compare the closing costs with the monthly savings to find your break-even point. If you plan to move before that point, refinancing may not be worthwhile.

Is there a penalty for paying off my mortgage early?

Most modern conventional and government-backed mortgages do not have prepayment penalties, and some rules restrict them. However, some loans, especially certain older or non-standard loans, may still include one. Always check your Loan Estimate and closing disclosure, or ask your lender directly, before you make large extra payments.

What is mortgage recasting?

Recasting means you make a large lump-sum payment toward principal, and the lender recalculates your monthly payment based on the smaller remaining balance, while keeping the same interest rate and end date. It can lower your required monthly payment without refinancing. Not all lenders offer recasting, and there is often a fee, so ask before you plan around it.

Should I pay extra on my mortgage or invest the money?

This depends on your interest rate, your other debts, your risk tolerance and your goals. Paying extra gives a guaranteed return equal to your interest rate. Investing can offer higher returns, but it also carries risk. Many people first build an emergency fund, take any employer retirement match, and pay off high-interest debt before adding extra to a low-rate mortgage. This is general information, so a financial advisor can help you decide.

What happens if I miss a mortgage payment?

Late payments usually trigger a late fee after a short grace period, often 10 to 15 days. A payment that is 30 days late may be reported to credit bureaus, which can lower your score. If payments are missed for several months, the lender can start foreclosure. Contact your servicer as soon as you know you may struggle, because hardship options or repayment plans may be available.

🙋 General questions

How is my monthly mortgage payment calculated?

The principal and interest part uses the standard formula: monthly payment equals the loan amount multiplied by the monthly rate, divided by one minus (one plus the monthly rate) raised to the negative power of the number of payments. Taxes and insurance are then divided by 12 and added. Our calculator uses this same formula and shows the results on the page.

How much of my early payments go to interest?

In the early years of a 30-year loan, most of each payment goes to interest. Over time, the share going to principal increases. This is why extra payments in the early years have a strong effect, because they reduce a balance that has the most interest still ahead of it.

What is a Loan Estimate and a Closing Disclosure?

A Loan Estimate is a standard three-page form a lender must give you within three business days after you apply. It shows the estimated loan terms, monthly payment and closing costs. A Closing Disclosure is provided at least three business days before you close, and it shows the final figures. Compare the two carefully, and ask questions about any changes.

Can I deduct mortgage interest on my taxes?

In some cases, you can deduct mortgage interest if you itemize deductions on your federal return. Current rules limit the deduction for larger loan amounts, and the rules can change. Your tax situation is unique, so please speak with a qualified tax professional before counting on any deduction.

Are the results from this calculator exact?

No. The results are estimates for planning. Your lender's figures may differ because of your final rate, PMI, fees, escrow changes, insurance premiums and local tax rates. Use the calculator to compare scenarios, then confirm the details with your lender and, for important decisions, a licensed professional.
